## KQ1. Commercial K-12 SIS — worldwide and Asia; which are used by Malaysian international/private schools; pricing where public

### Takeaway
The global K-12 SIS market is fragmented and dominated (in the US) by PowerSchool (~23% of identified implementations), FACTS, Infinite Campus and Skyward; the independent/international-school segment is served by a different set (Veracross, iSAMS, Engage/Double First, Blackbaud, Toddle, ManageBac) and Engage has a documented Malaysian user-conference footprint (Taylor's International School, 2019). Published pricing exists only for the SMB tier (Gradelink from USD 121/mo for ≤50 students; Classe365 from USD 100/mo for ≤100 students; RosarioSIS hosted from USD 29/user/mo), while enterprise vendors are quote-only. Malaysian domestic vendors ASIS (claims 3,000+ schools/tadika/tuition) and AOne (claims 4,000+ learning centres) are the incumbents your product will be compared against.

### Inferences
- For a small Malaysian private school or tuition chain, the realistic competitive set is ASIS, AOne, MyCampusSquare and CodeCanyon-derived scripts, not PowerSchool/Veracross; international schools in KL/Johor are largely locked into iSAMS/Engage/Veracross-type UK/US products with multi-currency fees and boarding modules.
- Public SMB price anchors cluster at roughly USD 1–2.50 per student per month (Classe365, Gradelink), i.e. about RM 5–12/student/month; a Malaysian product priced per-centre or per-branch (as AOne/ASIS appear to do) must justify itself against that.
- The PowerSchool breach (support-portal credential → tenant data) is a concrete design lesson: vendor support access must be scoped, audited and MFA-protected; Malaysian buyers increasingly cite PDPA.

### Inferences
- Reuse strategy: the best "read the schema, don't fork" references for a Laravel+React product are Unifiedtransform (largest Laravel community, clean domain split session→semester→class→section→course), skuul (multi-tenant/multi-school on Laravel 9 + MIT, the most permissive licence in the set) and academico (Filament admin patterns). GPL/AGPL projects (Unifiedtransform, Gibbon, RosarioSIS, CloudSchool) cannot be embedded in a proprietary SaaS without releasing source; MIT (skuul, lav_sms) and Apache-2.0 (Fedena, UniTime) can.
- Gibbon and RosarioSIS are the most complete feature references (behaviour, library, petty cash, billing, transcripts, eligibility) but are procedural PHP with their own module frameworks, not Laravel.
- Frappe Education/OpenEduCat are the only OSS options that come with real accounting/HR/payroll because they sit on ERPNext/Odoo; a Laravel product will need to integrate rather than replicate that.
- CodeCanyon scripts show what buyers in MY/ID/BD/IN expect on a checklist (hostel, transport, library, canteen, ID cards, admit cards, SMS gateway, multi-school SaaS), but repeated reviewer complaints about code quality mean they are not architecture references.

## KQ3. Malaysian public-sector systems (APDM, SAPS, DELIMa, Frog VLE history, PAJSK, e-Operasi): data fields, workflows, and export/API conventions a private system should mirror

### Takeaway
Malaysian public schools run on a constellation of KPM web apps: APDM (student master data + daily attendance), SAPS (school exam marks entry/analysis, seeded from APDM), PAJSK (co-curricular/sports/PE scoring), e-Operasi (teacher HR/placement) and DELIMa (Google-Workspace-based learning platform that replaced Frog VLE after the 1BestariNet contract ended 30 June 2019). There is no published public API; the de facto interchange format is Excel/CSV export from APDM and Excel templates (Maklumat/Markah/Subjek sheets) for SAPS. A private product should mirror APDM's student/guardian fields (incl. MyKid/IC, ethnicity, religion, guardian income/occupation, aid received) and SAPS-style subject-mark structures so data can be exchanged with teachers and parents who already know these forms.

### Cited Findings
- System glossary (from a KPM district office links page): APDM = Aplikasi Pangkalan Data Murid (student database); SAPS-Guru = Sistem Analisis Peperiksaan Sekolah; PAJSK = Pentaksiran Aktiviti Jasmani, Sukan dan Kokurikulum; SPLKPM = KPM training management; e-Operasi = teacher management module — [PPD Kuala Selangor links page](https://sites.google.com/btpnsel.edu.my/ppdksel/pautan) (search snippet)
- APDM: official KPM student data application used by schools to store and update student biodata, parent information, class, school and daily attendance records — [apdmpanduan.my](https://apdmpanduan.my/); [Bumi Gemilang APDM](https://www.bumigemilang.com/apdm-sistem-aplikasi-pangkalan-data-murid/) (search snippets)
- APDM data fields (as described by guide sites): attendance status (hadir / lewat / tidak hadir dengan sebab / tanpa sebab); student profile (name, ID/IC number, gender, ethnicity, religion, address); guardian (parent/guardian name, phone, income, occupation); assistance/aid received; export to Excel/CSV from Student Management or Reports section — [apdm-kpm.com](https://apdm-kpm.com/); [Cara ambil data APDM](https://apdm-kpm.com/cara-ambil-data-apdm/); [ecentral APDM](https://ecentral.my/apdm/) (search snippets; these are unofficial guide sites, not KPM)

- PAJSK-style co-curricular scoring (attendance, involvement, achievement, position) is a differentiator for private schools that want to mirror government-recognised records for university entry.

## KQ5. Timetabling/scheduling engines (aSc Timetables, FET, UniTime, Untis, Edval) and how they position automatic class allocation

### Takeaway
Automatic timetable generation is a solved-but-specialised problem: FET (AGPL-3.0, C++/Qt, v7.10.5 released 11 Sep 2026, has a command-line mode) is the only viable open-source engine for K-12; UniTime (Apache-2.0, Java, Apereo) is university-oriented; aSc Timetables and Untis are mature commercial desktop generators; Edval is a premium quote-based product aimed at large secondary schools with complex electives. Their positioning is "generate a conflict-free timetable from constraints, then hand-edit", not live allocation inside an SIS; SIS products (Gibbon, RosarioSIS, skuul, Unifiedtransform) only store/display timetables and leave generation to these tools.

### Cited Findings
- FET: free software implementing a heuristic algorithm to automatically schedule timetables for schools, high schools and universities; written by Liviu Lalescu and Volker Dirr; GNU AGPL v3; latest FET-7.10.5 released 11 September 2026 — [FET official site](https://lalescu.ro/liviu/fet/); [Wikipedia FET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FET_(timetabling_software)); [FET download](https://lalescu.ro/liviu/fet/download.html) (search snippets)
- FET 7.10.5 mirror: AGPL-3.0 code, GFDL-1.3 docs; builds with Qt6 (6.10+/CMake 3.28+) or Qt5 (5.15+/qmake); prebuilt Linux/Windows/macOS binaries; "full GUI and command-line-only versions" — [GitHub nico-alvz/fet-mirror](https://github.com/nico-alvz/fet-mirror) (fetched); upstream is not git-hosted (source tarballs only) — [GitHub zeeneddie/fet-timetabling](https://github.com/zeeneddie/fet-timetabling)
- Foradian (Fedena's maker) published a fork of FET for command-line (headless, no X-server) use — evidence that a commercial SIS vendor embedded FET as its auto-timetable engine — [GitHub foradian/FET](https://github.com/foradian/FET) (search snippet)
- FET man page describes it as "free timetabling" for educational institutions — [Ubuntu manpage FET](https://manpages.ubuntu.com/manpages/xenial/man1/fet.1.html)
- UniTime: "comprehensive educational scheduling system" for course and exam timetables, change management, room sharing with events; Apache-2.0; Java/GWT/Hibernate; 349 stars, last push 2026-09-28 (GitHub API); Apereo Foundation sponsored project since March 2015; five components: Course Timetabling, Examination Timetabling, Event Management, Student Scheduling, Instructor Scheduling; goal includes "minimization of student course conflicts"; distributed solver — [GitHub UniTime/unitime](https://github.com/UniTime/unitime) (fetched)
- aSc Timetables "automatically generates optimized school timetables handling teacher, room, and subject constraints"; Untis is "comprehensive timetabling software for schools and universities with multi-language support and real-time updates" with WebUntis cloud/mobile layer, widely used in Europe; FET "free open-source pick"; Edval "built for the complex end of the market — large secondary schools with heavy elective and option-line structures … premium, quote-based" — [Smootables: best school timetable software 2026](https://smootables.com/en/best-school-timetable-software-2026); [Gitnux top class scheduling software](https://gitnux.org/best/class-scheduling-software/) (search snippets)

### Inferences
- For a Laravel+React SIS, the pragmatic path is: store timetable as periods × class × teacher × room; export the FET XML input format and call the FET command-line binary (AGPL — running it as a separate process invoked by your app does not force AGPL on your code, but distributing a modified FET would); import the resulting timetable. Foradian's headless FET fork is precedent.
- Tuition chains rarely need combinatorial timetabling (fixed weekly slots, small teacher pools); the harder problem is capacity/allocation (student ↔ slot ↔ tutor with waiting lists), which none of these engines target and which is a differentiation opportunity.
- Edval/Untis/aSc pricing is per-school annual desktop licensing (quote-based for Edval); no figures captured.
