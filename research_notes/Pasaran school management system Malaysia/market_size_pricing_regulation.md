**Method / evidence-quality note for the report writer.** Research was run on 2026-09-29. The environment's egress proxy blocked *every* page fetch attempted (KPM/MOE, DOSM, MDEC, The Edge, Malay Mail, Capterra, SEC, vendor sites, law-firm sites, Wikipedia) and the WebSearch budget was exhausted mid-task. Every figure below therefore comes from **search-engine result snippets** that quote the linked page, not from reading the page itself. Treat items marked **[verified-snippet]** as "the linked page states this, per the search snippet" and items marked **[unverified]** as recall from training data or a secondary/aggregator claim that could not be checked. ## KQ1. How many tuition centres, tadika/taska, private & international schools and enrichment centres exist in Malaysia?

### Takeaway
No public, current national count of KPM-registered *pusat tuisyen* was found; the best hard number is DOSM's Economic Census 2023: **18,242 private-education-services establishments in 2022** (all sub-sectors incl. tuition centres, preschools, driving schools), up from 11,722 in 2015. Secondary sources put private schools at ~501 (of which ~240–256 international), KPM-registered tadika at ~7,655, and the largest preschool chains (Q-dees, Smart Reader Kids) at 180–200+ centres each.

### Cited Findings
- **Private education services establishments: 18,242 in 2022**, up 6.5% p.a. from 11,722 in 2015; gross output RM24.1 billion (2022) vs RM15.2 billion (2015), +6.9% p.a.; intermediate input RM9.1 billion (2022). Sector definition explicitly includes "tuition centre, driving school and other education" alongside pre-school, primary, secondary, TVET, tertiary, sports and cultural education. Reference year 2022, released Aug 2024. [verified-snippet] — [DOSM Economic Census 2023: Education Services Sector](https://www.dosm.gov.my/portal-main/release-content/economic-census-2023-education-services-sector); DOSM also publishes [Annual Economic Statistics – Private Education Services 2022](https://v1.dosm.gov.my/v1/index.php?r=column/cthemeByCat&cat=328&bul_id=ZzJiRlo0Z2JDelEvSS9UcGY5RE5Rdz09&menu_id=b0pIV1E3RW40VWRTUkZocEhyZ1pLUT09) and [Annual Economic Statistics – Education Services](https://www.dosm.gov.my/portal-main/release-content/5b27ce20-b764-11ed-9995-1866daa77ef9) which should contain the tuition-centre sub-sector establishment count (not retrievable here).
- **Private schools: 501 registered private schools in 6 categories — International 256, Private Primary 73, Private Secondary 73, Chinese Independent High Schools 60, Expatriate 20, Special Education 19; plus 141 private universities** (2026 directory). [verified-snippet, secondary directory source] — [EduSwasta](https://eduswasta.my/)
- **International schools: 240 (SchoolAdvisor "verified list 2026")**; another directory gives 193; EduSwasta gives 256. Counts differ by methodology/registration status. [verified-snippet] — [SchoolAdvisor](https://schooladvisor.my/international-schools); [ISC Research Malaysia report page](https://iscresearch.com/reports/malaysia/) (paywalled; figures not retrieved).
- **Tadika/taska: 7,655 preschools registered with KPM and 2,757+ taska registered with JKM** (aggregator sites; date not stated in snippet, likely 2024–25). [unverified – aggregator] — [CariSchool statistik](https://www.carischools.com/statistik.html); [SemakanStatus](https://semakanstatus.com/semakan-status-tadika-taska/). Official lists: [JKM Senarai Taska Berdaftar](https://www.jkm.gov.my/main/taska).
- Regulatory split: **TASKA** (under-4s) registers under the Child Care Centres Act 1984 with JKM; **TADIKA/TABIKA** (4–6) registers under the Education Act 1996 with KPM (or KEMAS/Perpaduan for government-run). [verified-snippet] — [KPWKM taska page](https://www.kpwkm.gov.my/portal-main/list-services?type=taman-asuhan-kanak-kanak); [KPM tadika guideline (MyGP)](https://www.mygp.gov.my/garis-panduan-penubuhan-pendaftaran-dan-pengoperasian-tadika-swasta-kementerian-pendidikan-malaysia)
- **Smart Reader Kids: "180 centres" (EduSwasta page title, 2026) / "more than 200 centres nationwide" (snippet body); fees RM250–650/month; ranks second only to Q-dees on centre count among Malaysian preschool brands.** [verified-snippet, secondary] — [EduSwasta Smart Reader Kids](https://eduswasta.my/preschool-chains/smart-reader-kids/); [Smart Reader centres](https://smartreader.edu.my/our-centres/)
- Kumon Malaysia operates a centre locator but no national centre count was surfaced. [verified-snippet, no number] — [Kumon Malaysia find-us](https://my.kumonglobal.com/find-us/)
- Private-education sector value added: **RM12.27 billion in 2023, ~0.8% of GDP, share constant over the last decade** (covers preschool to tertiary plus tuition centres, driving schools, other education). [verified-snippet] — [The Edge cover story](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/727019)
- KPM's Private Education Division (BPSwasta) publishes a "Lanskap" (landscape) page that should carry institution counts by category. [not retrievable] — [KPM BPSwasta Lanskap](https://www.moe.gov.my/bpswasta-lanskap)

### Inferences
- If ~501 private schools and ~7,655 KPM tadika are subtracted from DOSM's 18,242 establishments (2022), roughly **~8,000–10,000 establishments remain for tuition centres, enrichment/skill centres, language centres, driving schools, tertiary and "other education"** — so a plausible order-of-magnitude for KPM-registered tuition + enrichment centres is **several thousand (est. 4,000–8,000)**, before counting unregistered home tutors and pop-up centres. This is an inference, not a sourced count.
- DOSM's 6.5% p.a. establishment growth (2015–22) suggests the addressable base is expanding ~1,000+ establishments/year.
- Franchise preschool chains (Q-dees ~200+, Smart Reader ~180–200, plus Tadika Khalifah, Little Caliphs, Genius Aulad, Brainy Bunch etc. — chain counts unverified) are natural multi-branch SaaS targets, but many run HQ-mandated systems.

### Gaps
- **No official KPM count of registered pusat tuisyen (national or by state) was found.** Best routes: KPM BPSwasta "Lanskap" page, KPM Quick Facts, Parliamentary Hansard answers, or a request to Unit Pendidikan Swasta at each JPN (Johor and Sabah units have public portals: [JPN Johor UPS](https://sites.google.com/view/upsjpnjohor/perkhidmatan/permohonan-baharu/pusat-tuisyen), [JPN Sabah SPS](https://sites.google.com/moe-dl.edu.my/pendidikan-swasta-spsjpnsabah)).
- DOSM Economic Census sub-sector breakdown (tuition-centre establishments, revenue, employment) exists but could not be opened.
- No verified counts for Kumon, Eye Level, UCMAS, Q-dees; JKM taska national figure (2,757) looks low versus past JKM statements and should be confirmed against JKM's list.

## KQ2. Size and growth of the Malaysian private tuition / shadow-education market (with Singapore comparison)

### Takeaway
No credible RM figure for Malaysian *tuition-only* spend was found; the hard anchors are DOSM (private education gross output RM24.1bn in 2022; value added RM12.27bn in 2023) and participation studies (88% of KL primary pupils in one survey). Singapore is well measured: **S$1.4bn (2018) → S$1.8bn (2023)** household tuition spend per the Household Expenditure Survey.

### Cited Findings
- Malaysia private education gross output RM24.1bn (2022), +6.9% p.a. since 2015. [verified-snippet] — [DOSM Economic Census 2023](https://www.dosm.gov.my/portal-main/release-content/economic-census-2023-education-services-sector)
- Malaysia private education value added RM12.27bn (2023), 0.8% of GDP. [verified-snippet] — [The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/727019)
- Kuala Lumpur survey of 1,600 students in 8 schools: **88% of primary pupils used some form of private tutoring**; ~20% of households spent on private tutoring per the 2004/05 Household Expenditure Survey (old data). [verified-snippet] — [ADB, Bray & Lykins, "Shadow Education"](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/29777/shadow-education.pdf); [ERIC JICE 2017](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1210056.pdf)
- Household income is the strongest determinant of tutoring spend; urban households earning RM5,000–15,000/month are the primary tutoring market. [verified-snippet, secondary blog citing research] — [Champion Tutor](https://www.championtutor.my/shadow-education-in-malaysia-understanding-the-cultural-phenomenon/); academic: [Studies in Education 2024 (Malaysian secondary students)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/03055698.2024.2405815)
- **Singapore: households spent S$1.4bn on private tuition in 2018 and S$1.8bn (≈US$1.35bn) in 2023** (Household Expenditure Survey), reported by NIE/NTU and Bloomberg (June 2025). [verified-snippet] — [NIE/NTU](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/nie/news-events/news/detail/singapore-s-private-tutoring-boom-reveals-the-hidden-cost-of-success); [Bloomberg 6 Jun 2025](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-06-06/singapore-private-tutor-boom-spurs-fears-over-cost-and-student-burnout)
- A tuition-agency blog extrapolates S$2.14bn by 2025 (+19% over two years) — **projection, not data**. [verified-snippet, speculative] — [SmileTutor](https://smiletutor.sg/singapore-families-spent-1-8b-on-private-tuition-in-2023-heres-what-that-means-for-2025/)
- Global private-tutoring market reports exist (Fortune Business Insights to 2034) but are paywalled and not Malaysia-specific. [paywalled] — [Fortune Business Insights](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/private-tutoring-market-104753)

### Inferences
- Applying Singapore's ratio (S$1.8bn tuition spend for ~0.45m school-age children) to Malaysia's much larger cohort (~5m primary+secondary pupils) but lower incomes gives a wide, unsourced range; a founder should build bottom-up instead: (est. tuition centres) × (avg students per centre) × (avg fee). Example: 5,000 centres × 120 students × RM120/month ≈ RM0.9bn/yr — **illustrative only**.
- Software TAM is a small slice: at RM150–400 per centre per month across ~5,000–10,000 tuition/enrichment centres, the Malaysian tuition-software revenue pool is roughly **RM10–50m/year** (inference).

### Gaps
- No HolonIQ / Statista / Ken Research Malaysia tuition-market figure was retrievable; all such reports are paywalled.
- No Bank Negara / DOSM HES line item for "tuition" spend was found in this session (DOSM HES 2022 does publish education spend by household — worth checking).

## KQ3. Regulation: Education Act 1996, JPS registration, teacher permits, fees, PDPA (2024 amendments), e-invoicing, SST

### Takeaway
Tuition centres must register under s.79 Education Act 1996 via the State JPN (MOEIS system), hold teaching permits for every tutor (RM5,000 fine per s.133 without one) and worker permits for staff, and face up to RM30,000/2 years for operating unregistered. PDPA amendments took full effect **1 June 2025** (mandatory DPO, breach notification, data portability). E-invoicing: sub-RM500k businesses were exempted in June 2025, then the exemption threshold was reportedly lifted to **RM1m from 1 Jan 2026** and **RM3m from 1 Sept 2026** (latter needs confirmation). SST: 6% on private education only where fees exceed RM60,000/student/year (from 1 Jul 2025) — effectively irrelevant to tuition centres and tadika; SaaS itself is a taxable IT/digital service at 8%.

### Cited Findings
**Education Act / registration**
- All private educational institutions must register under **s.79 Education Act 1996 (Act 550)** at the State Education Department; establishment approval (kelulusan penubuhan) is obtained first via the **MOEIS** online system, then forwarded to Bahagian Pendidikan Swasta for registration. [verified-snippet] — [KPM BPSwasta FAQ](https://www.moe.gov.my/bpswasta-lazim); [JPN Johor UPS – Pusat Tuisyen](https://sites.google.com/view/upsjpnjohor/perkhidmatan/permohonan-baharu/pusat-tuisyen)
- Requirements: premises approval from local authority (PBT), Bomba and Health Office; submit names of managers, teachers and staff; meet classroom/subject requirements; pay registration fee. [verified-snippet] — [JPN Sabah SPS Garis Panduan](https://sites.google.com/moe-dl.edu.my/pendidikan-swasta-spsjpnsabah/garis-panduan); [JPWPKL summary of conditions (PDF)](https://jpwpkl.moe.gov.my/muat-turun/sektor-pengurusan-sekolah/unit-pendidikan-swasta/garis-panduan-penubuhan-institut-pendidikan-swasta/102-ringkasan-syarat-penubuhan-pendaftaran-kategori-pusat/file)
- **Teaching permits are mandatory for tutors; RM5,000 fine under s.133 for teaching without a permit; worker permits required for permanent staff; enforcement penalties up to RM30,000 fine or 2 years' jail or both.** [verified-snippet] — [KPM BPSwasta FAQ](https://www.moe.gov.my/bpswasta-lazim); [Mishu guide](https://mishu.my/blog/company-incorporation-and-formation/how-to-open-tuition-center-malaysia/); [Coach Mohd Noor guide](https://coachmohdnoor.com/bagaimana-ingin-membuat-pendaftaran-pusat-tuisyen-di-malaysia/)
- BPSwasta conducts spot inspections of tuition centres to verify tutors hold permits or relevant academic qualifications. [verified-snippet, social post] — [Threads post](https://www.threads.com/@hmdisa_/post/DX1pz0Gj9wg)
- Private tadika have a dedicated KPM guideline (establishment, registration, operation). [verified-snippet, content not read] — [MyGP guideline](https://www.mygp.gov.my/garis-panduan-penubuhan-pendaftaran-dan-pengoperasian-tadika-swasta-kementerian-pendidikan-malaysia)

**PDPA**
- The **Personal Data Protection (Amendment) Act 2024** was brought into force in phases (1 Jan 2025 onward), with the main new obligations effective **1 June 2025**: mandatory **Data Protection Officer** for controllers and processors, mandatory **breach notification** to the Commissioner "as soon as practicable" and to affected data subjects, and a **data portability** right (subject to technical feasibility). The PDP Department issued three guidelines: Data Breach Notification, DPO Appointment, and Guideline 03/2025 on Cross-Border Transfer. [verified-snippet] — [ASEAN Briefing](https://www.aseanbriefing.com/news/malaysia-tightens-data-protection-from-june-2025/); [Mayer Brown Jul 2025](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/from-legislative-reform-to-practical-guidance-key-amendments-to-malaysias-pdpa-and-the-launch-of-cross-border-transfer-guidelines); [DLA Piper Mar 2025](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2025/03/malaysia-guidelines-issued-on-data-breach-notification-and-data-protection-officer-appointment/); [Transatlantic Law](https://www.transatlanticlaw.com/content/malaysia-update-countdown-to-compliance-personal-data-protection-amendment-act-2024-in-force-1st-january-2025/); [Raja Darryl & Loh](https://rajadarrylloh.com/the-personal-data-protection-amendment-act-2024-and-guidelines-on-the-appointment-of-data-protection-officer-and-data-breach-notification/)
- [unverified – recall, confirm against DLA Piper/RDL links above] DPO guideline thresholds: DPO required where processing exceeds ~20,000 data subjects, or ~10,000 data subjects' sensitive/financial data, or regular systematic monitoring; breach notification to Commissioner within 72 hours; to data subjects within 7 days where "significant harm" (incl. sensitive data or >1,000 subjects); penalties raised to up to RM1 million and/or 3 years; data processors now directly liable for security principle.

**E-invoicing (LHDN)**
- **5–6 June 2025 IRB announcement: businesses with annual turnover below RM500,000 exempted from e-invoicing; the RM1m–RM5m group's mandate deferred to 1 Jan 2026.** [verified-snippet] — [Malay Mail 6 Jun 2025](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2025/06/06/irb-malaysia-exempts-businesses-below-rm500000-from-e-invoicing-delays-mandate-for-rm1m-to-rm5m-group-to-2026/179391)
- Per ClearTax's timeline page: on **6 Dec 2025 the Cabinet raised the exemption threshold to RM1 million effective 1 Jan 2026**; the threshold was **raised again to RM3 million effective 1 Sept 2026**; Phase 4 (turnover up to RM5m) applies from 1 Jan 2026 with relaxation to 31 Dec 2027; individual e-invoices mandatory for transactions >RM10,000 from 1 Jan 2026. [verified-snippet but single secondary source; the RM3m/1 Sept 2026 step must be confirmed with LHDN] — [ClearTax phases](https://www.cleartax.com/my/en/different-phases-implementation-timelines-einvoicing-malaysia); [ClearTax overview](https://www.cleartax.com/my/en/e-invoicing-malaysia); [Adventus update 22 Dec 2025](https://adventus-consult.com/e-invoicing/updates-20251222/); [Axxis 2026 update](https://axxis-consulting.com/lhdn-e-invoicing-2026-update/); [Info-Tech "RM1M exemption"](https://www.info-tech.com.my/blog/malaysia-new-e-invoice-guidelines/); [envoice "under RM3 million"](https://envoice.my/en/learn/e-invoice-under-rm3-million-malaysia)

**SST**
- **From 1 July 2025, education services by Education Act-registered institutions (private preschools, primary/secondary, international schools) attract 6% service tax only where charges exceed RM60,000 per student per academic year**; exemptions for Malaysian citizens, OKU cardholders, special-education schools and language centres; no minimum turnover threshold but only schools above the RM60k fee level must register; higher-education for non-citizens taxed at 6%. [verified-snippet] — [BMCC/TraTax](https://bmcc.org.my/tratax-new-sst-revisions-impacting-malaysias-private-education-sector/); [Aqran Vijandran guide](https://www.aqranvijandran.com/blog/malaysias-sst-expansion-from-1-july-2025-a-practical-guide-for-businesses); [Customs FAQ – Private Education Services (PDF)](https://mysst.customs.gov.my/assets/document/FAQ%20Industry%20SST/3.%20FAQs%20-%20PRIVATE%20EDUCATION%20SERVICES.pdf); [PIE News](https://thepienews.com/malaysias-6-private-education-tax-to-hit-international-students-and-partnerships/)
- Women, Family & Community Development Minister (30 Jun 2025): SST will not impact private kindergarten fees. [verified-snippet] — [Malay Mail 30 Jun 2025](https://malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2025/06/30/sst-will-not-impact-private-kindergarten-fees-says-womens-minister/182208)
- **Digital/SaaS services: 8% service tax** applies to digital services (SToDS for foreign providers once RM500,000 taxable sales; domestic IT services similarly at 8%), applies B2B and B2C; grace period to 31 Dec 2025 for businesses newly in scope from the 1 Jul 2025 expansion. [verified-snippet] — [Anrok Malaysia](https://www.anrok.com/vat-software-digital-services/malaysia); [Kintsugi SST guide 2026](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/apac/malaysia); [InCorp digital tax guide](https://malaysia.incorp.asia/guides/digital-business-tax-requirements-malaysia/); [Wolters Kluwer](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en-my/expert-insights/service-tax-sst-expansion-in-malaysia)

### Inferences
- A management-software vendor storing children's data is a **data processor** (and controller for its own records) under the amended PDPA: it should appoint/register a DPO, provide breach-notification tooling to centres (72h clock), and support export (portability). This is a sellable compliance feature for centres.
- Because most tuition centres/tadika turn over well under RM1m (many under RM500k), **most are e-invoice-exempt in 2026**; but multi-branch chains (>RM1m, or >RM3m if the Sept 2026 change is confirmed) must issue e-invoices — a strong reason for the SaaS to offer MyInvois-compliant receipts/consolidated e-invoices.
- The RM60,000 SST fee threshold means tuition centres and tadika do **not** charge SST on fees; the SaaS vendor itself must charge 8% service tax on subscriptions once registered (RM500k threshold for IT services — confirm).

### Gaps
- Exact tuition-centre registration fee amounts, permit fees, and whether **fee schedules (yuran) require KPM approval** were not confirmed (JPWPKL PDF not readable). Recall [unverified]: registration fee is modest (tens to low hundreds of RM), and fee schedules must be displayed/declared to JPN rather than "approved".
- PDPA DPO thresholds and breach timelines listed above are from recall and need confirmation.
- The RM3m e-invoice exemption from 1 Sept 2026 rests on one secondary source (ClearTax snippet) and conflicts with the RM1m figure quoted by other 2026 guides — verify on LHDN MyInvois portal.

## KQ4. How local and regional vendors price (AOne, Malaysian micro-vendors, Singapore, Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand) and centres' willingness to pay

### Takeaway
Malaysia's category leader AOne (AOneSchool) does not publish prices; Singapore benchmarks put centre-management software at **S$2–10 per student per month or S$300–1,500/month feature tiers**, Schoolber at **S$2/student/month**, and Indonesia's Telkom-backed Pijar Sekolah at **Rp16,000–20,000 (~RM4.5–5.5) per user per month** with installation waived on promotion. No Malaysian willingness-to-pay survey was found.

### Cited Findings
- **AOne / AOneSchool** (Malaysia; also aoneschools.sg in Singapore) positions as "Malaysia's #1 learning centre management software" for enrichment/tuition centres, kindergartens, academies, swimming and dance schools; modules: AOnePay automated fee collection, fee tracking/receipts, class scheduling and booking, attendance and progress reporting, teacher management and payroll, parent/teacher apps. **No public price list**; Capterra profile exists (pricing "contact vendor"). [verified-snippet] — [AOne](https://aone.com.my/); [AOne products](https://aone.com.my/products/); [AOne SG](https://aoneschools.sg/); [Capterra AOneSchools](https://www.capterra.com/p/190964/AOneSchools/); [AOne blog](https://blog.myaone.my/general/aoneschool-management-software/)
- Other Malaysian tuition-centre systems surfaced (pricing not retrievable): Tuis.my (web + Microsoft Store app), EduTenant, yuran.my (fee management), SimTrain (has a Malaysia pricing page), Skooly, Illumine (lists "10 best school management systems in Malaysia"). [verified-snippet, prices unknown] — [Tuis.my](https://www.tuis.my/); [EduTenant](https://www.edutenant.online/); [yuran.my](https://yuran.my/pengurusan-yuran-untuk-pusat-tuisyen/); [SimTrain MY pricing](https://www.simtrainsystem.com/?q=pricing-my); [Illumine list](https://illumine.app/blog/school-management-systems-malaysia)

- **Schoolber (SG): S$2 per student per month**, unlimited courses, for schools, enrichment and tuition centres. [verified-snippet] — [Schoolber](https://www.schoolber.com.sg/school-management-software.html)
- **Indonesia – Pijar Sekolah (Telkom): Rp20,000/user/month list; Rp20,000 (3-month), Rp18,000 (6-month), Rp16,000 (12-month) per student; Indibiz promo "from Rp400 thousand/month" with free installation.** [verified-snippet] — [Pijar Sekolah subscription](https://pijarsekolah.id/blog/cara-langganan-pijar-sekolah-telkom); [Indibiz Telkom on X](https://x.com/Indibiz_ID/status/1723027934947033252); Indonesian pricing-model guide: [Seqolah 2026](https://www.seqolah.com/blog/panduan-biaya-aplikasi-pembayaran-sekolah-2026)
- Malaysian tuition *fee* levels (for ARPA context): private/home tuition from RM30/hr; tutors report RM30–45/hr online for secondary. [verified-snippet] — [GoLearn 2026 rates](https://golearn.com.my/tuition-rates); [Threads tutor post](https://www.threads.com/@mas_masturina96/post/DNfW_YOJ0iO/)

### Inferences
- Regional norm for SME centres is **usage-based per-student pricing (~RM4–10/student/month equivalent)** or **flat tiers (~RM200–1,500/month)**, often with payments-processing take-rate on top (AOnePay model). A Malaysian centre with 100 students paying RM5/student ≈ RM500/month — likely at the upper end of tolerance given RM100–200/month typical tuition fees.
- Vendors that bundle **fee collection (FPX/DuitNow/e-wallet)** monetise via payment margin, allowing low headline SaaS prices; this is AOne's apparent model.

### Gaps
- No public AOne price; no LittleTree (could not identify a Malaysian vendor by that name — may be an internal codename or misremembered brand); no Kelas Pintar, Base.vn (Vietnam) or Thai vendor pricing found.
- **No Malaysian survey of centres' willingness to pay for software** was found. Suggest primary research (10–20 owner interviews) — the single biggest gap for pricing.

## KQ5. Unit economics for vertical SaaS in this space (ARPA, CAC, churn) from public comparables

### Takeaway
Public signals: Procare (childcare SaaS + payments) sold to Roper for **US$1.86bn (Feb 2024)**; Brightwheel is estimated at **~US$37.5m ARR (2024) on a US$735m valuation, US$88.8m raised** (third-party estimate); India's Teachmint grew revenue 4.3x to **Rs 74 crore in FY25** while cutting losses 57% — but both Teachmint and Classplus **pivoted away from pure tutor-SaaS** (to hardware/smart classrooms and test-prep respectively), a cautionary signal on SMB-tutor SaaS economics. No ARPA/CAC/churn disclosures were found.

### Cited Findings

## KQ6. Government digitalisation incentives a centre could use to buy software (Geran Digital PMKS Madani, MDEC, BSN, SME Corp) and whether a school-management SaaS can be a listed provider

### Takeaway
**Geran Digital PMKS Madani 2025: 50% matching grant up to RM5,000 per MSME, RM50m allocation, administered by MOF/MDEC/BSN/MCMC.** MSMEs must be ≥60% Malaysian-owned, registered (SSM/PBT/SKM), ≥6 months operating, ≥RM50,000 average annual turnover, and first-time recipients. To be a **Digitalisation Partner (DP)** the vendor must be an active **MD/MSC-status company ≥51% Malaysian-owned** (or a Ministry of Digital agency) — so a school-management SaaS can qualify only after obtaining Malaysia Digital status.

### Cited Findings
- Grant: 50% of approved digital service cost, **up to RM5,000 per company; RM50m allocation under Budget 2025**; administered by MOF, MDEC, BSN and MCMC. [verified-snippet] — [Kiizen guide](https://www.kiizen.com.my/msme-digital-grant-2025/); [Presoft](https://presoft.com.my/msme-digitalisation-grant/); [Beanse](https://beanse.com/geran-digital-pmks-madani/)
- **MSME eligibility: ≥60% Malaysian-owned; registered with SSM, PBT or SKM; operating ≥6 months; average annual turnover ≥RM50,000; not a previous Digitalisation Matching Grant recipient (unless applying solely for e-invoicing).** [verified-snippet] — [Kiizen](https://www.kiizen.com.my/msme-digital-grant-2025/); [Asia Pacific](https://www.asiapacific.com.my/geran)
- **DP requirements: active MD/MSC-status company compliant with requirements, OR an agency under the Ministry of Digital; ≥51% Malaysian-owned.** [verified-snippet] — [MDEC – Join as Digitalisation Partner 2025](https://www.mdec.my/announcement/digitalisation-partner-PMKS)
- Telcos (e.g., Maxis) and software vendors run campaigns tied to the grant; some vendors advertise a **2026 edition** ("Geran PMKS Madani 2026 | 50% matching grant"). [verified-snippet, vendor pages; 2026 details unconfirmed] — [Maxis T&C](https://www.business.maxis.com.my/en/terms-conditions/campaigns/gdpm/); [mesraHR 2026](https://www.mesrahr.com/ms/geran-pmks-madani); [DreamzTech](https://www.dreamztech.com.my/en/news/msme-grant-2025); [iBiZZ](https://ibizzcloud.com/geran-digital-pmks-madani-2025/)

### Inferences
- A tuition centre with ≥RM50k turnover and 6+ months of operation can claim 50% of a first-year subscription up to RM5,000 — enough to cover a RM200–400/month plan for 1–2 years, materially cutting effective CAC for the vendor if it is a listed DP.
- Path for the founder: obtain **Malaysia Digital (MD) status** via MDEC, then apply as DP under the relevant category (ERP/CRM/e-invoicing/digital payments); bundling e-invoicing and digital payments improves category fit.
